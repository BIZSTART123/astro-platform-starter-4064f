## Full recap (newsletter / website / email)

### Caribbean Fest 2026: Faith, Fellowship and Caribbean Joy

This past Saturday, Team Winter brought Caribbean Fest 2026 to Bible Truth Church of God International in the Bronx. The night celebrated Jamaican heritage through worship, culture and community, and the room was full of all three.

**Dressed for the occasion.** The sanctuary was decked out in green, orange and gold. Drapes were swagged across the stage beneath the cross, golden mums lined the front, and Jamaican flags hung throughout. Guests came ready too, in madras plaid, yellow head wraps, Jamaica-flag earrings and plenty of black, green and gold.

**Honouring Miss Lou.** The centrepiece of the décor was a large banner honouring Louise Bennett-Coverley, the beloved *Miss Lou*. She was Jamaica's poet, folklorist and cultural icon, and she championed Jamaican Patois and folk culture on the world stage. The banner also showed the island's national symbols: the Doctor Bird (national bird), Lignum Vitae (national flower), Ackee (national fruit) and the Blue Mahoe (national tree).

**Wisdom from home.** A second banner shared well-known Jamaican proverbs, each with its meaning:
- *"One one coco full basket"*: little by little, the basket gets full.
- *"Every mikkle mek a mukkle"*: small things add up to plenty.
- *"Wi likkle but wi tallawah"*: we may be small, but we are mighty.
- *"Chicken merry, hawk deh near"*: when times are sweet, stay watchful.
- *"Wah fi yuh cyaan be un-fi yuh"*: what God has for you is for you.
- *"Wanti wanti cyaan get it, getti getti nuh want it"*: value what you have; someone wishes for it.
- *"Wah sweet nanny goat a go run him belly"*: what tastes sweet today can hurt tomorrow.

**On the program.** Backed by a live band, the night moved between:
- **Spoken word and dramatic performances.** Performers in madras plaid and dark jackets held the stage. One walked down into the congregation to work the crowd, in true Miss Lou storytelling style.
- **Solo ministry.** A vocalist sang from centre stage.
- **The vocal group.** This was the high point of the night. In green Jamaica jackets, bright print skirts and plaid, they brought the whole house to its feet.
- **Worship.** The congregation stood with hands raised in praise. It was a reminder that the celebration was about faith as much as culture.

By the end, people were dancing in the aisles and waving flags. Many stopped by the Caribbean Fest **Tuck Shop** before heading home.

**Thank you.** To everyone who performed, played, decorated, served, gave and showed up: thank you for the love. As Miss Lou would remind us, *"one one coco full basket."* Every contribution, big or small, made this night what it was.
